# Genre "dialogue" — characters talking to each other

1. **Whoever speaks is seen.** During a `<d>`, the speaker's face is in frame, front or three-quarter: a speaker seen from behind or off-screen loses lip-sync. A voice-over is the exception, and is written as such.
2. **Every `<d>` carries its speaker** `(S1)`, `(S2)`… in the shot that says it, reused at each turn: this is what links the reference voice to the speech.
3. **Cover the exchange with cuts**: shot, reverse shot, the listener's reaction, a cut to what the speech provokes. An exchange filmed from one point of view lacks drama.
4. **Breathe.** A margin of at least 2 s before the first syllable and after the last; do not fill the silence with action.
5. **The camera stays discreet**: simple movements, alternating shot sizes, a background that does not move behind a close-up.
6. **A voice only heard** (narrator, voice-over) is not an image reference: the image shows what the voice evokes; the voice is described by a stable description followed by `(Sx)`.
