# Genre "montage" — a series of brief moments that answer each other

The whole plan is a sequence: **one shot per moment**, each about 2 s, often in different places or with different subjects. The idea linking them is what holds the whole together.

1. **One moment = one shot = one iconic image.** Pick the instant that reads in 2 s, not a complete action: the impact, the gesture at its peak, the look. No subplot inside a shot.
2. **Every shot is complete.** It names its subject, what it does, its framing; it does not assume the previous one. A distinct framing at each cut.
3. **Link with a visual rhyme**: a shape, a movement, a colour, a screen direction that passes from one shot to the next. Without it, the moments read like a list.
4. **One reference per moment**, six images at most: order them like the moments; what needs no image is said in prose.
5. **Open wide** if the montage must situate the world; tighten afterwards. Aim for 1.5 s per shot at least (cadence rules, section 2).
6. **Sound is a single thread**: the same ambience (`overall_soundscape`) and the same musical bed over the whole series; a sound impact may punctuate a cut, written in the shot where it happens. No dialogue in the middle of a flash-cut, except a voice-over laid over the whole.
7. **The scene mood is held**: same time of day, same general light, unless a moment expressly says otherwise.
