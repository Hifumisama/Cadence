# Genre "action" — writing a physical gesture or a clash

This scene is action: what the body does must be **readable**. A spectacular effect with no gesture producing it gives a frozen character in front of a moving set.

1. **Whole body first.** Describe the footing (feet, hips, shoulders), then the limb that strikes or guides. A readable chain: **preparation → impulse → effect → recovery**. "He bends his knees, his weight shifts to the front leg, the arm shoots straight, the impact lifts the dust" turns into an image; "he masters the element" does not.
2. **Use the brief's gesture.** If the character has a `gestuelle` (fighting style, trade, gait), translate it into precise **postures and movements** (low stances and grounded strikes, spins and dodges, or the heavy gait of a craftsman at work…). "A dance", "graceful movements" are empty words: the model renders them as hand gestures.
3. **The wide shot shows the gesture; the detail comes after.** A close-up on a lone hand, without the body that threw it, makes the gesture tiny. Open on a frame showing the body and the effect, then cut to the detail.
4. **The effect follows the gesture, never precedes it.** Matter (water, stone, flame, cloth, dust) reacts **after** the impulse and continues its direction. Describe its matter, direction and speed.
5. **One readable event per shot.** One shot = one gesture or one impact. Intense cadence (one shot every 1.5–2 s), varied angles (ground, high-angle, canted).
6. **The character sheet stands alone**: effects and props are said in prose (or come from an effect asset), not from the character's image.
